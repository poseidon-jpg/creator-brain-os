## 2. DEEP DIVES (best 4)

### Deep Dive 1: The originality wall (sparks 3, 4, 32). The constraint that shapes the whole editor

What TikTok's own pages say (VERIFIED, UK Seller University pages plus US Policy Pulse):
- "Reproduced and Unoriginal Content" for short videos prohibits lightly modifying another creator's video with text, filters or new audio, recreating a video shot for shot, and AI content that "closely duplicates others (same tool, prompt, avatar, or template)". Reposting your own video is "discouraged as repetitive"; uploading the same pre-recorded content to multiple accounts is prohibited. Enforcement ladder: warning, product removal, content removal, suspension, permanent commission freeze.
- "Avoiding Duplicated Content": acceptable variation = trim a few seconds, change start/end point, add filters or different background music, re-edit with a different focus, make a summary version, make a follow-up video. Same-product videos have no specific rule but TikTok suggests changing focus (testimonial into reply-to-comment).
- US Policy Pulse: "Layered videos, frame insertion, and format disguise are prohibited" and any attempt to conceal violations through editing "is strictly prohibited". The US creator guide asks for "a new script and ideas for each video".

What it means for the editor (INFERRED): the monetizable unit is a different angle from the same footage, not a pixel-shuffled copy. One raw session should yield maybe 3 to 6 genuinely different cuts (different hook, different story order, different proof beat, reply-to-comment framing), posted on one account, spaced out. It should not yield 150 near-identical files. The Helium10 guest's 50x5x3 batch (spark 5) is a brand-side seller with his own shop; applying it as "150 near-duplicates from one clip" is exactly what TikTok's duplicate policy targets. Treat 150 as an upper bound on combinations, and gate output with a similarity check (see R21). Conflict between this wall and the "3 to 10 posts per day" folklore is unresolved by any source I found; the platform ceiling for new creators is about 3 e-commerce videos per day (spark 2).

### Deep Dive 2: What data actually exists on volume vs yield (spark 7, 26, 27)

Bullseye Sellers' census (MEASURED, shop-side, 121 shops, 28-day window ending Aug 2026): average videos per shop and GMV per video by GMV tier. T5 ($100K+) averaged 573 videos and $305.49 per video; T1 averaged 76 videos at $18.87. Affiliate share of GMV 57.5 to 79.2 percent. The author attributes T3's high per-video yield ($232.56) to operations, noting the highest refund rate (7.2 percent), and says creators convert better on shops with sales history and reviews.

What it does NOT tell us: it is brand-side, not affiliate-side; it does not say what is in the videos; one portfolio, one window. Takeaway for design (INFERRED): yield per video depends heavily on the product's sales history and reviews, so the system should rank products before it ranks edits. Her own GMV-per-video log is the only dataset that can answer "which hooks sell" for her, which argues for a learning loop (tag every posted video with hook archetype, format, length, product category, then join to Seller Center GMV per video).

### Deep Dive 3: Hook and structure, what is actually authoritative vs folklore (sparks 1, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12, 18)

Authoritative: Academy says hook = first 3 to 6 seconds, product-related; key message; CTA spoken or on-screen (VERIFIED).
Folklore that clusters (SAID, many vendors repeat it, none give method): decide within about 2 to 3 seconds; open on the result; show the product in use within 2 s; text and stickers off the product; three or more hooks per body; hook archetypes (result-first, confession, specific number, objection, comparison, sound-only).
Conflicts to resolve with her own tests: Trifecta hook "need not mention the product" vs Academy "must relate to the featured product"; contrarian-first vs the 8,758-video memo showing archetypes within about 0.13 points of engagement (UNVERIFIED, search summary only). Recommendation: encode archetypes as rotatable, tagged variables, not as a ranked list.

### Deep Dive 4: Compliance as lintable rules (sparks 20 to 25)

The US Creator and Content Policy Guide gives an editor-checklist-shaped ruleset (VERIFIED through the fetch summarizer): do not alter product appearance or specs; flag AI segments; no copyrighted clips or songs; strip external watermarks; add "Results may vary" for before/after or time-based claims; remove absolute, medical, weight-loss and GLP-1 language; keep at least 3 seconds of dynamic footage between still segments; remove off-platform links, QR codes and contact info; verify displayed prices. These map almost one-to-one to automated checks. Two items need a human: the disclosure toggle (posting screen) and picking a CML sound (in-app).

## 3. PLAYBOOK RULES an editor can encode

Each rule: ID. Rule. (Source spark; label.) Defaults are starting hypotheses to A/B, not truths.

**Structure and hook**
- R1. Every cut follows Hook, Key message, CTA. (Spark 1; VERIFIED)
- R2. Hook window = first 3 seconds of footage is the hook payload; hard maximum 6 s. Within it the product (or its result) must appear. (Spark 1; VERIFIED for 3 to 6 s and product relation)
- R3. Generate hook variants as tagged archetypes: result-first, problem-first, specific-number, objection/contrarian, comparison, confession, sound-first. Log the tag per video. Rotate; do not rank until her data exists. (Sparks 10, 12; SAID)
- R4. Three layers in the opening: spoken line (from her raw audio via transcription), on-screen headline text within the first 3 s, and a visual (product action or result). Text withholds the full payoff. (Spark 18; SAID)
- R5. Default total length 15 to 45 s; per-format defaults: demo 15 to 30, before/after 15 to 25, problem-fix 20 to 40, review 30 to 60, unboxing 30 to 60. Output two lengths for each winner candidate. (Spark 12; SAID. Helium10 guest: 7 s to 3.5 min worked, SAID)
- R6. Hands-only footage longer than about 20 s must have a voice or face overlay or be cut shorter. (Sparks 8, 12; VERIFIED for TikTok preferring people on camera, SAID for 20 s)
- R7. End with a spoken or on-screen CTA every time ("tap the cart below" style); if raw audio lacks one, add on-screen text. (Sparks 1, 6; VERIFIED structure, SAID wording)

**Cuts, captions, audio**
- R8. Cut dead air and pauses; trim start and end tight; minimal editing beats heavy editing. Cut count is NOT standardized by any source: log cuts per second as a metadata field and let her data decide. (Spark 6; SAID. Gap: no source gives a cut count)
- R9. Burn in word-level captions; headline text in first 3 s; keep captions and text out of the bottom fifth and right edge (product card and buttons zone) and off the product itself. (Sparks 6, 12, 19; SAID, with the same safe-zone logic in the MIT `ecommerce-video-skills` renderer)
- R10. Loudness normalize to about -14 LUFS, clean noise, keep the natural product sound in the first second. (Sparks 19, 33; SAID; the -14 figure comes from the MIT repo README)
- R11. Never export with music from the general library. Default: no music bed baked in, or a CML-cleared track; instruct her to select "Commercial Sounds" in the app. Anything unlinkable later if non-CML. (Sparks 13, 22; VERIFIED for Link Products rule, SAID for broader terms)
- R12. Vertical 9:16, 1080x1920. (Convention; INFERRED, no source fetched specifying this)

**Product integrity and quality**
- R13. The real product must be shown in motion; never replace with stock, illustration, or AI-rendered product. Match the linked listing. (Spark 17, policy; VERIFIED)
- R14. At least 3 seconds of continuous dynamic footage at any point; no static, slideshow, screen-recording or looping segments as the main content; keep people or hands visibly interacting. (Sparks 8 and Deep Dive 4; VERIFIED)
- R15. No color grade or filter that changes the product's apparent color, size or effect; before/after must not alter lighting of the "before" or add beauty filters. (Spark 23; VERIFIED)
- R16. Any AI-edited element (synthetic voice, generated b-roll, face changes) sets a "needs AI disclosure" flag in the run report; permitted AI edits are lighting, background that leaves product information intact, translation, subtitles, dubbing. No AI-generated voice that fakes a testimonial. (Spark 20; VERIFIED guide, UNVERIFIED date details)

**Compliance linting (caption and transcript)**
- R17. Block list on captions and spoken transcript: absolute claims ("100%", "guaranteed", "instant results", "no side effects"), medical cure/treat/prevent claims, weight-loss promises, GLP-1 mentions, "lowest/cheapest price", competitor disparagement, external links, handles, QR codes, phone numbers. Flag, do not silently rewrite. (Spark 23; VERIFIED)
- R18. For before/after or time-bound claims auto-add "Results may vary" text. (Spark 23; VERIFIED)
- R19. Output a posting checklist per video: turn on "Disclose commercial content" (Paid partnership), add the product link in the app, choose a Commercial Sound, run the Video Pre-Check Tool. The editor cannot do these; the run report must prompt them. (Sparks 13, 21, 24; VERIFIED)

**Variants and originality**
- R20. A "variant" must differ in at least hook text and spoken hook AND in angle (story order, proof beat, or reply-to-comment framing), not only in captions or color. (Deep Dive 1; INFERRED from VERIFIED policy)
- R21. Add a similarity gate: do not export two variants of the same clip that share more than a set fraction of identical footage in the same order. Threshold is a design choice with no source; start strict and tune. (INFERRED; UNVERIFIED threshold)
- R22. Never apply layering, inserted frames, overlays meant to defeat detection, mirroring or other "format disguise". (Spark 3; VERIFIED)
- R23. Never produce the same file for multiple accounts; one account per variant family. (Spark 4; VERIFIED)
- R24. Cap on-pipeline output per source session to a few genuinely different cuts and honor the daily posting ceiling for her account tier (3 e-commerce videos per day for new creators; up to 30 for eligible accounts). (Spark 2; VERIFIED)
- R25. Preserve a clean, un-captioned, un-watermarked master per video, because sellers may boost it via GMV Max (affiliate post authorization). (Spark 14; VERIFIED)
- R26. Strip third-party watermarks and logos; do not re-use other creators' clips; do not reproduce a reference video shot for shot (reference-teardown tools like `viral-remake` copy structure only). (Spark 4 and 19; VERIFIED policy)

**Learning loop**
- R27. Tag every posted video with hook archetype, format, length, cut density, caption style, product category, CTA wording, and join it to Seller Center GMV per video. Judge by GMV per video, not views. (Sparks 7, 27; INFERRED, MEASURED shape for volume vs yield)
- R28. Use factorial rotation: 3 hooks x 1 body first, then promote winners (the Helium10 guest reports a 2 to 3 percent hit rate, so expect most to fail). (Spark 5; SAID)
