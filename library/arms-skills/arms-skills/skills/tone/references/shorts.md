# Shorts - the spoken register for reels, Shorts and TikToks

The short-form branch of the spoken register. A reel is one point in under a minute, so long-video habits (section transitions, "stick around", callbacks) are dead air here. Read this instead of the long-form spoken principles for any short script, then apply the user's spoken voice from SKILL.md on top.

## The shape

1. **Hook**: one sentence that stops the scroll.
2. **Gloss**: if the thing isn't a household name, say what it is in the next sentence ("X is basically a small add-on that..."). Nothing after it lands until the viewer knows what the thing is.
3. **The beats**, one per paragraph. The how is mandatory and fits in one line: the prompt in quotes, the setting, the click.
4. **The payoff**, its own paragraph, and it works as an ending on its own, because some platforms cut before the ask.
5. **The ask** (optional): one clear action, like "comment GUIDE and I'll send it". The keyword names the payoff in one word.

## Rules of thumb

- **Length**: about 120 to 190 words for 30 to 50 seconds at roughly four words a second. Never past 60 seconds.
- **One beat per paragraph**, a blank line between, because each paragraph is one breath and one cut when reading off a teleprompter.
- **One point per sentence.** Still full spoken sentences, never slide fragments.
- **Numbers as words** when read aloud ("seventy thousand stars"), so the reader doesn't stumble.
- **Gloss jargon inline**, keep the payoff true and modest, and say a caveat only when it changes what the viewer does next.
- **Left out**: section transitions, callbacks, "near the end I'll show you", analogies (unless the analogy is the whole reel).

## Reel checklist

- [ ] The hook stops the scroll in one sentence
- [ ] A gloss straight after the hook when the thing needs one
- [ ] One beat per paragraph, one point per sentence, every sentence one a person would say
- [ ] The how, in one line
- [ ] The payoff ends the reel on its own, then the ask
