# Copy masters - six voice modes for posts

Six well-known ways to write a post, named after writers whose style shows each one. Pick ONE mode that fits the post type, then write it in the user's own voice from SKILL.md. Never mix all six into one post. The master rules at the bottom apply to every post.

The named writers are inspirations for a style, not endorsements; none of them made or reviewed this file.

These are general copywriting principles. If the user's own samples disagree with a rule here, the samples win.

## 1. The Simplifier

**Use for:** announcements, pricing, anything where clarity is everything.

**Core idea:** clear beats clever. Write so a child could read it.

- The first line is the point. Never warm up.
- Cut every word that doesn't move the reader forward. If you can remove a sentence and the meaning stays, remove it.
- Short sentences beat long ones. Simple words beat fancy ones.
- State a benefit in the negative when it hits harder ("Don't start without this" over "Use this when starting").
- One idea per post.
- A bold claim in line 1, the proof in lines 2 and 3.

## 2. The Engager

**Use for:** challenges, participation asks, anything that wants replies.

**Core idea:** make it easy to join in. Lower every barrier.

- The hook is line 1 of the body. No intro.
- Bullets are specific ("your desk setup", not "your workspace").
- One unexpected or fun bullet makes people smile and reply.
- Add a "no wrong answers" line so nobody feels judged.
- "Show me" framing feels exciting, not clinical.
- Post your own answer first to set the tone.
- Close in one line.

## 3. The Host

**Use for:** welcome posts, community updates, tips, value shares.

**Core idea:** make everyone feel they belong, the new member and the veteran alike.

- Keep a signature opener and closer, so posts feel like they come from one person.
- Frame value as shortcuts, not lessons.
- Mention specific members by name when it fits, so people feel seen.
- Watch the curse of knowledge: explain what feels obvious to you.
- Casual, but every sentence earns its place.

## 4. The Architect

**Use for:** teaching posts, list posts, anything with a framework.

**Core idea:** every post has a structure. Value, then format, then action.

- The hook stops the scroll. It interrupts a pattern, it doesn't warm up.
- Name a problem everyone hates, then take it apart together.
- Disrupt a belief, rebuild it with facts, then point to the fix.
- Teach something the reader can use in under a minute.
- End with a recap or a question that makes replying easy.
- Pick one narrow reader per post.

## 5. The Philosopher

**Use for:** opinion posts, mindset shifts, deeper ideas.

**Core idea:** a short post is attention plus one of problem, amplification or gain.

- Line breaks are emphasis. One thought per line.
- Start punchy (the first 2 or 3 lines short), then let sentences breathe.
- Break the pattern: if everyone writes lists, tell a story, and the other way round.
- Share a real opinion. Let people who disagree filter themselves out.
- Deep ideas, said simply. Never academic.

## 6. The Conversationalist

**Use for:** personal updates, relationship posts, honest moments.

**Core idea:** if it sounds like a copywriter wrote it, rewrite it.

- Read it aloud. Would you say this to a friend over coffee? If not, rewrite.
- Write to one person, not "everyone".
- No formula openers ("Who else wants...", "I'm excited to announce...").
- Shorter is almost always better.
- A consistent voice beats a clever one-off.

## 7. Master rules for every post

**Structure**
1. The first line is the hook or the ask. Never context first.
2. Short paragraphs with air between them.
3. At most 5 to 7 lines before a call to action or a break.
4. Bullets are specific and concrete.
5. One pattern-break bullet when it fits (humour, a surprise, a personal touch).

**Voice**
6. The read-aloud test on every line.
7. Write to one reader.
8. Casual words over formal ones ("btw" over "additionally").
9. State a benefit in the negative when it hits harder.

**Announcements**
10. Lead with what the reader gets, not what you're doing.
11. Price and deadline in the first 3 lines.
12. One call to action per post. Two actions confuse people.
13. Real scarcity only. Never fake urgency.
14. End on the easiest possible action ("reply YES", "tap the link").
