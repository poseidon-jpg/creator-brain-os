The Expedition

The desert does not photograph what you see. Under infrared film it photographs what it hides — the water still moving inside every living thing.

Why aerochrome?

Kodak Aerochrome III was a Cold War reconnaissance film built to expose camouflage from the air: healthy vegetation reflects infrared violently and burns onto the emulsion as pink and red, while dead matter stays grey. Discontinued in 2009, fewer than a few hundred rolls survive in freezers around the world. We carried twenty-six of them.

In September 2025, photographer Isolde Ferrer and a two-person crew drove 640 kilometres from Swakopmund into the dune sea of the central Namib — one of the driest places on the planet, and therefore the cruellest possible test for a film that only responds to life.

The gamble was simple. Where almost nothing lives, whatever does live should detonate on the frame. A single !nara melon vine crawling over a dune. A camel-thorn tree drinking from a fossil aquifer forty metres down. In ordinary photographs these things disappear into beige. On aerochrome they scream.

Every exposure was an act of faith: the film cannot be metered reliably, cannot be reviewed, and cooks above 24 °C. Daytime surface temperatures reached 51 °C. The freezer ran off the truck battery, and twice the truck battery ran off luck.

Twenty-one rolls came home. Two hundred and thirty-one frames survived development. Fourteen became MIRAGE.

Field Log

Day 01

Swakopmund → Solitaire. Freezer holding at −18 °C. Tomas insists the C14 road "has opinions". He is correct.

Max 34 °C
Day 03

First exposures at dawn on the dune sea. Wind under 5 knots for exactly forty minutes. Shot Roll 07 in thirty-eight of them.

Max 41 °C
Day 05

Truck battery flat at 05:20. Freezer silent for 3 hours. Rolls 14–16 written off as heat-fogged; developed anyway out of stubbornness. Two frames survived. One is Plate VI.

Max 51 °C
Day 08

A true mirage on the Tsauchab pan at noon — an inverted lake, trembling. The one thing the film cannot record is the thing we named the series for.

Max 47 °C
Day 11

Sesriem Canyon, final roll. Isolde exposed the last frame on the walk out and refused to say of what. It did not survive development. She says that's correct too.

Max 37 °C
