THE TWILIGHT ZONE

200 – 1,000 m

Sunlight fades to a blue whisper. Here begins the largest animal migration on Earth: every night, trillions of creatures rise from this zone to feed, and sink again before dawn.

At 09:12 the Lanternfish II crossed the thermocline. Water temperature fell six degrees in ninety seconds. Commander Vasquez logged the first flashes of living light off the port viewport - brief, cold, blue-green.

Dangles a glowing lure on a whip three times its body length. Prey swim to the light; the darkness underneath has teeth.

Zone 03 · Bathypelagic

THE MIDNIGHT ZONE

1,000 – 4,000 m

No sunlight has ever reached this water. Every point of light you see from here down was made by something alive.

The hull ticked as it shrank under pressure. At 2,300 metres the sonar picked up a slow-moving mass, forty metres long, that returned no echo twice in the same shape. Vasquez named it Contact Theta and did not follow it.

1,800 m

Vampire Umbrella Squid

Vampyroteuthis umbra

Neither squid nor octopus. When threatened it inverts its webbed arms over its head and vanishes into its own cloak.

2,600 m

Sleeper Grenadier

Coryphaenoides somnus

Cruises at glacier speed to save energy. Its heart beats eleven times per minute - one meal can last it a month.

3,700 m

Chandelier Siphonophore

Erenna lucerna

Not one animal but a colony of hundreds, strung like a chandelier. It fishes with twitching red lights - the only red in a blue world.

Zone 04 · Abyssopelagic

THE ABYSSAL ZONE

4,000 – 6,000 m

A plain of soft grey ooze wider than every continent combined. It snows here, constantly - a slow drift of organic dust called marine snow, the only food that ever arrives.

Pressure outside the viewport is now more than 400 atmospheres: the weight of a small car pressing on every square centimetre of the hull. The Lanternfish II's titanium sphere was built for twice that. Vasquez tapped the glass anyway. Everyone does.

4,700 m

Dumbo Octopus

Grimpoteuthis meridiana

Flies through the dark by flapping two ear-like fins. The deepest-living octopus known - and, the crew agreed, the most cheerful thing at 4,700 metres.

5,200 m

Tripod Spiderfish

Bathypterois araneus

Stands motionless on three stilt-like fins, facing the current with its mouth open. It is functionally blind. It waits. It always waits.

5,800 m

Glass Sea Cucumber

Enypniastes vitrea

Completely transparent - you can watch its last meal travel through it. Locals of the research fleet call it the "headless chicken monster".

Zone 05 · Hadalpelagic

THE HADAL TRENCH

6,000 – 10,935 m

Named for Hades. Below six thousand metres the ocean stops being a layer and becomes a place: narrow trenches carved where one tectonic plate dives beneath another.

At 14:58 the Lanternfish II settled onto the floor of Horizon Deep. Depth 10,935 metres. Pressure 1,094 atmospheres. Outside the viewport, in the beam of the lamp: a pale snailfish, swimming by, entirely unbothered. Life was here first.

8,100 m

Hadal Snailfish

Pseudoliparis hadalis

Soft, scaleless, faintly pink - the deepest fish ever filmed. Its cells are packed with a molecule that stops pressure from crushing its proteins flat.

9,700 m

Giant Trench Amphipod

Alicella hadessa

A shrimp-like scavenger the size of a house cat's paw. Swarms of them strip a fallen whale carcass to bone in the dark, ten kilometres down.

10,900 m

Trench Glass Garden

Hyalonema abyssi

Sponges anchored by spun-glass stalks, swaying in currents that take a thousand years to cross the trench. The oldest may predate the Roman Empire.
