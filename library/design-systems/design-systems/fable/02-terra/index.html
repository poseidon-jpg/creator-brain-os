The atelier

Fifteen summers at the wheel

TERRA began in 2011 when Eleni Mavrou left a ceramics restoration post at the Benaki Museum and rented a collapsed goat shed above Lefkes. She rebuilt it stone by stone, dug her first clay from the marble-flecked slope behind it, and fired her first forty pots in a kiln made from salvaged brick. Thirty-one survived. She still keeps the nine that didn't.

Today the atelier is Eleni, her partner Nikos, and two apprentices each season. Everything is thrown by hand from Parian stoneware blended with our own wild clay. Glazes come from what the island gives: olive-wood ash, marble dust, iron sand from Molos beach.

"Clay remembers everything you do to it. Our work is simply to give it good memories."

— Eleni Mavrou, founder

From slope to shelf

Four slow steps, no shortcuts

Dig & wedge

Wild clay is dug each October from the eastern slope, soaked for six weeks, sieved twice, then wedged by foot the old Cycladic way.

Throw

Every form is thrown on one of two kick wheels. No molds, no jigger arm. A large amphora can take three sittings over two days.

Glaze

Ash from our olive prunings is washed, ball-milled, and blended with marble dust. Each batch shifts with the season's wood.

Fire

The courtyard kiln climbs to 1,180°C over fourteen hours. We fire eight times a year, always starting before dawn.

Firing-day picnics

Eight times a year the studio closes and the courtyard opens. Bring nothing; we set a long table with garden food, island wine, and a front-row seat to the kiln. Dates are announced to the letter list first.
