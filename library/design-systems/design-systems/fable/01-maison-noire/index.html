01 The Maison

We believe darkness is not an absence. It is the richest fabric we have ever touched.

Maison Noire was founded in 2011 by Hélène Marchand, formerly première d'atelier at two of the great houses of Avenue Montaigne. She left with a single conviction: that a house could devote itself entirely to the spectrum between ink and candlelight, and never exhaust it.

Every season the maison presents exactly fifteen looks. Nothing is produced in advance. Each garment is cut for one client, in one fitting room, under the north light of Le Marais.

Our ateliers keep a library of 214 shadow tones, each dyed in-house and archived under seal: encre, suie, minuit, cendre froide, or éteint. A Maison Noire black is never flat. It moves.

03 The Atelier

Four hands, one needle, no hurry.

Behind a plain door in Le Marais, twenty-two artisans keep couture time. A single gown may pass through four hundred hours before it is permitted to leave the building.

I

The Toile

Every garment begins twice. First in cotton toile, draped directly on the client's form and corrected by Madame Marchand herself. The toile is archived forever; the client's shape becomes part of the maison's memory.

2 fittings
II

The Dye House

Our blacks are built, not bought. Cloth is dyed in the cellar in small copper vats, layer over layer, until it holds depth under candlelight and severity under noon sun. Some baths take eleven days.

214 archived tones
III

The Cut

One cutter per garment, always. Chief cutter Émile Rousseau works with shears made for the house in 1987 and refuses all others. The bias is checked by ear: good satin, pulled true, sounds like breath.

1 cutter per garment
IV

The Finishing Room

Hems weighted with hand-cast chain, buttonholes sewn with thread waxed at the bench, our petites mains sign the inside seam in gold silk. Invisible to everyone except the person wearing it.
