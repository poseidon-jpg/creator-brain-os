MITS Altair 8800

1975 · Albuquerque, NM · Intel 8080

The kit that started the fire. No screen, no keyboard · you talk to it in toggle switches and it answers in blinking LEDs. Ours runs a hand-keyed Kill the Bit, daily at 11:00.

Apple II

1977 · Cupertino, CA · MOS 6502

The first computer that looked like it belonged in a house instead of a lab. This unit shipped to a dentist in Fresno who used it for a single spreadsheet for eleven years. It plays Oregon Trail now.

IBM 5150 PC

1981 · Boca Raton, FL · Intel 8088

Big Blue's reluctant classic. Beige, serious, built like office furniture. The clone wars it accidentally triggered defined the next forty years of computing. Runs VisiCalc and Flight Simulator 1.0.

Commodore 64

1982 · West Chester, PA · MOS 6510

Still the best-selling single computer model ever made · roughly 17 million bread bins. The SID chip inside is why every synth nerd in the building keeps drifting to this corner. Try the joystick.

Macintosh 128K

1984 · Cupertino, CA · Motorola 68000

The machine this museum is named after a year of. A computer with a face, a mouse, and opinions. Our unit still has the original owner's MacPaint doodles on the internal disk. We kept every one.

DEC PDP-11/70

1975 · Maynard, MA · The room-heater

Not personal, not sorry. Our largest resident, on loan from Ferngate Polytechnic, where it computed payroll until 1993. UNIX v6 boots from tape on the first Saturday of each month. Bring earplugs.
